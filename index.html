Our Products

Fresh bananas, graded for export

Conventionally grown (non-organic) fruit, harvested at the right maturity for long-haul sea freight.

Flagship

Cavendish Banana (G9 / Grand Naine)

The world's most traded banana variety. Long, uniform fingers with a bright yellow peel after ripening, firm creamy flesh and excellent shelf life — ideal for supermarkets, wholesalers and ripening centres.

  • Finger length: 18 cm and above (as per buyer spec)
  • Calibre: 38–46 (as per buyer spec)
  • Shipped green (mature, unripe) for controlled ripening
  • Clusters / hands, crown-treated & packed with poly liner

Robusta Banana

A sturdy Cavendish-group variety with thick peel and sweet flavour, well suited for Middle East wholesale markets.

Yelakki (Elaichi) Banana

Small, aromatic and very sweet baby banana — popular with South Asian communities around the world.

Nendran / Plantain

Large cooking banana used for chips and traditional cuisine. Available against confirmed orders.

Red Banana

Distinctive red-purple peel, rich flavour. A premium speciality line available seasonally on request.

From Farm to Port

Our quality process

Every step is designed to protect the fruit through a long sea voyage.

  1. 01

    Farm selection

    Partner farms with good agricultural practices, monitored for bunch care, residue management and harvest timing.

  2. 02

    Harvest & transport

    Bunches harvested at export maturity and carried with padding to the pack-house to prevent bruising.

  3. 03

    De-handing & washing

    Hands cut into clusters, latex removed in washing tanks and fruit inspected for defects and scars.

  4. 04

    Grading & treatment

    Graded by length and calibre; crowns treated to prevent crown rot during transit.

  5. 05

    Packing & pre-cooling

    Packed in lined cartons, palletised and pre-cooled to bring pulp temperature down before loading.

  6. 06

    Reefer shipment

    Loaded into pre-cooled reefers at 13–14 °C with full documentation and shipment tracking shared with you.
